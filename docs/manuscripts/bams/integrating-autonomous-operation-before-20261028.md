Integrating autonomous operation
Power management is part of experimental design for an autonomous observatory. Keeping every instrument running continuously may exhaust the reserve before an important observing period, while reacting only when the battery is nearly empty leaves little room to plan. For AURORA, we have developed a forecasting framework that combines measured system behaviour, weather information and explicit observing priorities to assess operating choices before they are made. The central innovation is connecting these elements so that the energy available over coming days can inform which measurements are feasible and when.
The foundation is a realistic account of what the observatory consumes. Measurements from the Iceland deployment show that demand changes with instrument combinations, startup, heater and blower activity, and aircraft recharging. Essential computing, communications and surface measurements also draw power when the main remote-sensing instruments are off. These observations make clear why a single average load or a list of manufacturer ratings cannot adequately describe the energy required to sustain the science. [[1]](https://docs.google.com/document/d/167FYmHPFyTaQTacYCw7SwVHv5CzlpKqM4_a5-Rg1KAE/edit?usp=drivesdk)
We represent the observatory through a finite-state model: a set of identifiable equipment configurations, each with its own energy demand. Power measurements identify which instruments are operating, and statistical learning updates the expected demand of individual components and their combinations. Within a configuration, the model also learns recurring phases such as startup and heater operation. This distinction matters because switching an instrument on is an operator decision, whereas its heater may cycle automatically. Forecasts therefore follow the confirmed equipment configuration, while allowing demand to vary through its observed internal phases. [[2]](https://github.com/GAMB2LE/aurora_cloud_dashboard/blob/7399c08a60e37fa2b7b48b21369b1bfb6ac54c1d/docs/analysis/power-load-forecast-model.md)
Statistical learning therefore has a practical role in autonomous observing: characterising how the real system behaves and improving the information available for decisions. The learning is constrained by measured equipment states and the station energy balance. Repeated operating episodes establish the variability expected within each state, while new measurements update the estimates as conditions change. The resulting descriptions remain interpretable, so an operator can understand why the predicted demand has changed and identify behaviour that needs investigation. [[2]](https://github.com/GAMB2LE/aurora_cloud_dashboard/blob/7399c08a60e37fa2b7b48b21369b1bfb6ac54c1d/docs/analysis/power-load-forecast-model.md)
Weather forecasts supply the other side of the energy balance. The GAMB2LE forecasting system uses ECMWF predictions of incoming solar radiation, calibrated against station measurements, to estimate future electricity generation. This calibration connects forecast solar radiation to the electrical output of the particular installation. The generation forecast is combined with the latest measured battery charge, learned demand and estimates of usable battery capacity and efficiency. An ensemble of possible weather conditions, power demands and battery responses produces a range of future charge levels. Regular updates start again from the observed charge and current equipment state, keeping the forecast aligned with the observatory. [[2]](https://github.com/GAMB2LE/aurora_cloud_dashboard/blob/7399c08a60e37fa2b7b48b21369b1bfb6ac54c1d/docs/analysis/power-load-forecast-model.md)
Planning then compares specific operating choices, including maintaining the current configuration or running different combinations of the ceilometer, cloud radar and microwave radiometer. The present advisory scheduler prioritises sustained ceilometer observations, adding radar and radiometer operation when the remaining energy reserve permits. It plans the next four days while checking the reserve through a ten-day horizon, with minimum run lengths and limits on repeated starts. A conservative battery-reserve criterion helps avoid schedules that appear feasible only under favourable conditions. These priorities are explicit and can be reconsidered as the scientific objectives develop. [[3]](https://github.com/GAMB2LE/aurora_cloud_dashboard/blob/7399c08a60e37fa2b7b48b21369b1bfb6ac54c1d/docs/runtime/cl61-automation.md)
A next step is to use forecasts of scientific opportunity alongside forecasts of energy supply. For Greenland, this could mean conserving discretionary power before an anticipated period of clouds or precipitation, then supporting coordinated measurements through the event. The relevant choice would be which combination of observations best addresses the question, for how long, and with what reserve remaining afterwards. A targeted observing period would also need sufficient background measurements to interpret the event and assess any sampling bias. Event-specific scientific targeting remains a development objective; the current scheduler implements instrument priorities and energy constraints.
The present forecasting system provides advice and comparisons for operators. Automatic instrument switching is a separate development step, with decisions checked locally and subject to operator control. Fresh battery and equipment information and instrument-specific operating limits are essential to that connection. Reliable operation requires checking the age and consistency of the measurements used to initialise each forecast, and retaining a complete earlier forecast when an update cannot be produced reliably. The intended controller must distinguish a requested change from a confirmed electrical response, and electrical power from successful scientific acquisition. This staged approach allows forecast and scheduling behaviour to be examined before granting the software authority over instruments. [[3]](https://github.com/GAMB2LE/aurora_cloud_dashboard/blob/7399c08a60e37fa2b7b48b21369b1bfb6ac54c1d/docs/runtime/cl61-automation.md)[[4]](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WnTO_QARCv2WgVCnU32zOkfzk3xL_F_SE3L4zRzCuTg/edit?usp=drivesdk)[[6]](https://github.com/GAMB2LE/aurora-cloud-infra/blob/dddb6d72816b1b7b01e08f375e05b1b5958fe56b/docs/POWER_FORECAST_CONTINUITY_INPUTS.md)
Learning must also extend to the forecasts themselves. Archived forecasts are compared with subsequent observations to identify errors in predicted generation, demand and battery charge. Separating those errors helps determine whether an improvement is needed in the weather input, the representation of an instrument state or the battery model. Candidate improvements are evaluated separately before adoption. This provides a practical foundation for maintaining useful observations when energy is limited and, ultimately, allocating scarce power to the periods when those measurements matter most.
