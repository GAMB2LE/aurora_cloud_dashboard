# Integrating autonomous operation

Power management is part of experimental design for an autonomous observatory. Keeping every instrument running continuously may exhaust the reserve before an important observing period, while reacting only when the battery is nearly empty leaves little room to plan. For AURORA, we have developed a forecasting framework that combines measured system behaviour, weather forecasts and explicit instrument priorities. It connects the energy available over coming days to choices about which measurements are feasible, when to collect them and how much reserve to retain.

The foundation is a realistic account of station demand. Iceland observations show changes with instrument combinations, startup, heater and blower activity, and aircraft recharging. Computing, communications and surface measurements also consume power when the main remote-sensing instruments are off. Whole-station energy balance and individual outlet measurements therefore provide complementary information that manufacturer ratings or a single average load cannot supply. [1]

We represent the observatory through a finite-state model: a set of identifiable equipment configurations. Fresh outlet measurements help establish which instruments are powered, while statistical learning updates the demand of components and their combinations. Within each configuration, recurring operating episodes reveal startup durations and lower- and higher-power phases, such as the ceilometer's heater/blower cycle. The model distinguishes an operator's decision to switch an instrument on from subsequent automatic changes within that instrument. Forecasts hold the confirmed configuration unless an explicit schedule changes it, while allowing demand to vary through its learned internal phases. Sparse or ambiguous evidence invokes fallback estimates rather than inventing a well-learned state. [2]

Weather forecasts supply the generation side of the balance. The deployed baseline uses ECMWF predictions of incoming solar radiation, including a 50-member weather ensemble. An empirical conversion, calibrated against measured electrical generation and corrected for forecast lead time, estimates the output of the installed solar array. A more detailed model of the east-, south- and west-facing arrays, including solar geometry and conversion losses, has been implemented as an experimental candidate; its hardware assumptions and performance still require validation. Interfaces for comparing other weather-model inputs also remain experimental; the operational power forecast does not combine multiple weather models. [2][3]

Generation and demand feed a battery energy-balance model initialised from the battery management system's reported state of charge. It represents effective usable capacity, charging and discharging losses, power limits and the finite storage range. New forecasts return to the latest reported charge and equipment state. Ensemble trajectories combine weather differences, learned load variability and bounded variation in battery parameters. Their spread makes uncertainty visible, but remains conditional on the operating assumptions; it has not been shown to provide a calibrated probability of uninterrupted operation. [2][7]

Supply assumptions also need to follow the actual installation. Recent grid-charging work adds measured charger output to the inferred station balance, avoiding confusion between external supply and reduced demand. Although this correction is implemented, its operational forecast integration is not yet complete. A newer comparison between continued grid charging with solar disconnected and solar operation without grid charging is still a proposal. It must explicitly identify hypothetical solar reconnection and use calibration from a verified connected-array period. [4][7]

The implemented advisory scheduler prioritises sustained ceilometer observations, then allocates remaining energy to cloud radar and microwave radiometer operation. It plans four days while checking the reserve over ten days. New runs normally last at least 12 hours, with at most one start per instrument per UTC day. The scheduler screens choices against a conservative ensemble reserve threshold and can report that no acceptable schedule exists. These rules express a scientific preference for continuity and complementary measurements within the available energy budget; they do not identify meteorological events or establish a guaranteed safe reserve. [5]

Operators can also assess specified start times, durations and instrument combinations. This connects energy forecasting to the scientific value of a later observing period: discretionary operation can be postponed to retain charge for coordinated measurements when they matter most. For Greenland, an anticipated cloud or precipitation episode could motivate such a choice. Automatically identifying and ranking these opportunities, and deciding how much background sampling to retain, remain development objectives. Background observations are needed both to interpret an event and to assess the sampling bias introduced by selective operation. [5][7]

Forecast-driven instrument switching is a separate integration task. Prototype CL61 control and observe-only machinery exist, but the reviewed deployment does not establish an active forecast-to-instrument control loop. The latest simplified controller remains a design. It would check fresh local measurements, operating limits and operator permissions before acting, and require an expired forecast request to resolve to off. A requested switch must be distinguished from confirmed electrical response, and electrical power from successful scientific acquisition. Forecast freshness and complete, internally consistent products are prerequisites for useful advice as well as control. [5][6][7]

Archived forecasts and subsequent observations support evaluation of generation, demand and charge errors separately. A separate historical-research workflow extends this with experimental solar and load models and chronological replay. These candidates have not yet completed independent validation, so short development replays should not be taken as evidence of improved forecast skill. The present contribution is an interpretable connection between observed operating behaviour, forecast energy and explicit observing choices, providing a basis for conserving power for scientifically valuable periods. [3][7]
